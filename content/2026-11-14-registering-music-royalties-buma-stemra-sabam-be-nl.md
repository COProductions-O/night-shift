# Registering Your Original Music and Collecting Royalties in Belgium and the Netherlands: Practical 2026 Guide for Guitarists and Home Producers

If you write original guitar parts, songs, or produce tracks in Belgium or the Netherlands, money can appear every time that music is played live, streamed, broadcast, or used in a shop or café. Most of that money never reaches you unless you register the work with the right collective management organisation (CMO). This guide covers the practical steps for guitarists, singer-songwriters, and home producers on both sides of the border in 2026.

## Two Different Rights: Authors’ Rights vs Neighbouring Rights

There are two main streams:

- **Authors’ rights** (copyright) go to the composers, songwriters, and publishers. In the Netherlands this is handled by Buma/Stemra. In Belgium it is SABAM.
- **Neighbouring rights** (related rights) go to the performers (the musicians who played on the recording) and the producers (the people who financed or own the master). In the Netherlands this is Sena. In Belgium the performers’ side is PlayRight and the producers’ side is SIMIM.

A guitarist who wrote the riff and also played on the recording can receive both. A pure session player who did not write the music only receives neighbouring rights. If you release your own demos or singles, you are often both writer and producer.

## Netherlands: Buma/Stemra and Sena

**Buma/Stemra** collects for composers and songwriters when music is performed live, played on radio/TV, streamed, or used in public. Membership is individual (you cannot join as a band). From 1 January 2025 the first year is free for new members. After that the annual contribution is €63.55 for music creators (deducted from your payouts). Join at bumastemra.nl, sign digitally, then register each work in MijnBumaStemra. You need the title, correct copyright shares (list every writer even if they are not members), duration, and an audio file (WAV preferred) so the work can be fingerprinted. Register early — before the track goes online or is performed — because matching and claims can take time. You also submit setlists for live performances so Buma can pay you for your own gigs.

**Sena** handles neighbouring rights for performers and producers when recordings are played on radio, TV, or as background music. Membership is free (Sena takes a small percentage on distributions). Register your repertoire in MySena. If you self-release, you claim both the performer share (50 %) and the producer share (50 %).

When is it worth it? Buma/Stemra suggests membership if your work is regularly performed live, played on radio/TV, released commercially, or streamed more than roughly 200 000 times a year on paid platforms. For many apartment guitarists the first year is free, so the barrier is low once you have a few original tracks that get played.

## Belgium: SABAM, PlayRight and SIMIM

**SABAM** is the main authors’ rights organisation. Membership is a one-time cost: €75 administrative fee plus a share in the cooperative (€31 partial share that lets you attend meetings, or €124 full share that also gives voting rights). There is currently no annual fee. SABAM recommends joining once your works are actually being used (public performance, broadcast, streaming). Before that you can deposit works for free via OnlineDepot to establish the creation date and authorship. Once a member, register works in MySabam / MyWorx. Metadata (title, writers, featured artists, etc.) must match exactly what you submit to streaming platforms, otherwise royalties cannot be matched and paid.

**PlayRight** collects neighbouring rights for performers (musicians). **SIMIM** does the same for producers/master owners. Both work with Unisono for the equitable remuneration collected from broadcasters and public venues. Register your performances and recordings with PlayRight so distributions can reach you. As with the Dutch system, self-released artists can claim both sides.

## Practical Steps for Guitarists and Home Producers

1. Decide which rights you actually own. List every original song or instrumental: who wrote the music, who wrote lyrics (if any), who played on the recording, and who owns the master.
2. Choose the organisation that matches your residence and the rights you hold. Dutch residents normally use Buma/Stemra + Sena; Belgian residents use SABAM + PlayRight/SIMIM. You cannot be registered with two CMOs for the same rights and territory.
3. Join first (or deposit via OnlineDepot if you are not ready for full membership). Then register every work as soon as it is finished. Upload a clean audio file.
4. Keep metadata consistent. The title, writer names, and credits you put on Spotify/Apple Music must match the CMO registration exactly.
5. For live shows of your own material, submit setlists to Buma (Netherlands) so performance royalties can be claimed.
6. Check statements regularly. Distributions are often delayed; missing or mismatched data is the most common reason money stays unclaimed.

## Common Pitfalls and What to Skip

- Waiting until a track is already online. Register before release.
- Forgetting co-writers or incorrect percentage shares. Fixing these later is slow.
- Assuming a pure cover band generates author royalties for you. Covers pay the original writers, not the cover band (neighbouring rights may still apply to the recording if you made one).
- Ignoring neighbouring rights if you only play on other people’s tracks. Session work can still generate Sena or PlayRight income.
- Treating this as a replacement for a professional studio or mixing engineer. Clean, well-produced masters simply make registration and later commercial use easier.

## How This Fits Studio and Lesson Work

When clients bring original songs into a studio session in Belgium or the Netherlands, the engineer or producer often ends up with production credits or co-writing shares. Clear registration habits protect everyone and make it easier to split royalties correctly later. For guitar teachers working with adult songwriters, pointing students toward the correct CMO is a concrete next step once they start recording demos. Typical local studio rates (€50–100+/h) are better spent on performance and tone than on sorting out missing registrations after the fact.

Start with the official portals (bumastemra.nl, sena.nl, sabam.be, playright.be). The portals change slowly, but the core rule does not: if the work is not registered with the correct organisation, the money collected for its use cannot be paid to you.

For guitarists and producers in dense BE/NL cities who already record quiet demos at home and occasionally book studio time, this is one of the higher-leverage administrative steps you can take. It turns original guitar parts and songs into potential long-term income instead of files that only live on your hard drive.
